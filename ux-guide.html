How to read the lab

Start with a question, follow one connection, then inspect the accounting. Interfaces 2.1 and 2.2 add explanations around the existing 2.0 economic engine. It does not change the economic equations or establish that their assumptions are empirically correct.

One useful journey

  1. Choose a plain-language question. Read what the example changes before pressing Run guided example. Guided examples replace the draft with synthetic inputs; Undo restores your previous inputs.
  2. Read the reference and alternative as two complete conditional paths. The same selected year is used throughout the mechanism and outcome bridges. The year-zero valuation is separately labelled.
  3. Choose one stage of the mechanism map. Inspect its reference value, alternative value, difference, rule type and equation. Previous and Next connection move through the explanation without changing the simulated year. On narrow layouts, selecting a stage brings its explanation into view; Return to map restores the selected stage.
  4. Use the time control to look before, during and after a shock. A pulse ending restores its parameter rule; prices, capital, deposits, debt and expectations retain their history. Actual nominal purchases can still differ after the rule returns.
  5. Read the earnings bridge, then the earnings/multiple bridge. Open the equation detail for the arithmetic check. Use the accounting and market views when you want the complete records.

What the arrows mean

The map organises the implemented mechanisms for inspection. It does not establish a unique chronological sequence, an empirical causal graph or a measured contribution of each policy. Household spending, income and realised output are jointly solved in each period. Investment, prices, hours and rates adjust through the stated rules and delays.

The feedback note is deliberately specific: disposable income and positive net liquid wealth can enter spending. Equity revaluation does not enter consumption in this closure. A market mark is not newly produced output or immediately spendable cash.

What the bridges establish

Net earnings equal corporate gross surplus less depreciation, net business interest and corporate tax. The first bridge reconciles their signed changes. It works even when profits become losses.

When earnings and equity values are positive and known, equity value equals earnings multiplied by P/E. The second bridge divides the interaction equally between the earnings and multiple terms so that both contributions sum exactly to the total value difference. This is an arithmetic decomposition, not a causal attribution. The multiple includes the future cash path, growth, reinvestment and required returns; it is not a pure interest-rate or sentiment measure. Invalid comparisons are withheld.

Keep the units and dates straight

GDP, wages, purchases and profits are annual flows. Debt, deposits, capital and equity values are stocks at a date. Real output and net real hourly wages remove the model price level. Other money measures are nominal model units, with an opening reference GDP scale near 100. A larger nominal number does not necessarily buy more goods.

Equity today is the value at year zero of the whole announced dividend path. Equity at year five is the remaining conditional value at year five. Neither is an observed future market quote.

Read and compare the charts

Choose Values for the native units, Index at start for a common opening reference of 100, or Difference for alternative minus reference. A common reference preserves opening differences between cases. A nonpositive or missing opening value cannot support an index; choose Values or Difference. Rates, ratios, paid hours and the real hourly wage index keep their native denominators. Differences in rates are percentage points. Issuer charts keep the issuer’s reporting currency and are not divided by national population.

Each time chart labels its units, reference and alternative line styles, selected-year values and difference. Horizontal rules use rounded ticks. The vertical marker follows the shared selected year; click a chart or focus it and press the arrow keys, Home or End. Hover previews a year without changing the selected year. The gray intervals show the recorded shock schedule: a pulse is active inside its interval, a step persists, and a ramp’s shading marks its build before it holds. These intervals do not estimate causal effects. Paths coincide where one line covers the other.

Open Data and export for the complete displayed series, its units and transformation, CSV data and a standalone SVG. Missing observations remain gaps, including unsupported valuations. Y axes expand to the data and need not begin at zero; Difference views include zero.

Per capita and purchasing power

The Per capita view separates total real output from population, then plots real output, consumption, combined household disposable income, combined household wealth, corporate earnings and public net liabilities per capita. Every real amount uses its own path’s price level, and each path is divided by its own population index. The model’s labour field is the population index; workforce, retirement, participation and employment are separate. Values are model units per population-index unit, not currency per actual resident. An indexed per-capita series equals 100 × (amount / population) / the opening reference’s (amount / population).

Output equals population multiplied by output per capita. Growth factors multiply: 1 + output growth = (1 + population growth) × (1 + per-capita growth). The selected-date comparison between cases is separate from growth within a case over time. Run population comparison prepares an illustrative slower-growth case, with Undo available.

The model has no count of people or households inside each ownership group. Household averages therefore combine the groups; they are not medians, group averages or per-household estimates. Wealth includes conditional market marks. Public liabilities per capita are an accounting scale, not a forecast tax bill for each resident.

Why the interface is organised this way

The design exposes status, uses familiar questions, keeps Undo visible, and lets readers reveal specialised controls and equations when needed. These choices draw on Nielsen’s usability heuristics and progressive disclosure. They are design hypotheses; this release has not been evaluated in a study of novice users.

How to judge whether it is easier

A reader should be able to identify what changed, distinguish a flow from a stock, explain a finished pulse’s remaining effects, reconcile a profit difference, and say why a multiple change is not just sentiment. Test those tasks with new users, count mistakes and unnecessary navigation, and compare their explanations with the implemented rules. Successful rendering and arithmetic tests do not by themselves establish comprehension.
