Money institutions and reform plans

Fixed-quantity money, stable purchasing power, full reserve backing and a ban on lending are distinct proposals. The dynamic reserve experiment uses matched public reserves and Treasury funding; the transition ledgers below separate the Fisher and Benes–Kumhof illustrations.

Fisher 100% Money

A payment bank holds reserves against transaction deposits. A separate investment fund has risk-bearing units. This illustration gives the public issuer fund units in exchange for reserve money; no opening net wealth is gifted.

Benes and Kumhof

Public reserves replace deposit funding, with Treasury credit as the counterpart. Bank-held bills can be offset; private debt relief has a public counterpart. Compare interest-bearing debt with the broader balance sheet.

These are original accounting illustrations. They do not reproduce the authors’ full models or establish the authors’ simulated macroeconomic gains. The paper is research by its authors, not an IMF policy endorsement.

Hard nominal-spending thought experiment

If nominal spending M × V is held constant, a larger real output requires a lower price level. That is a conditional identity. Fixed base money alone does not fix velocity, bank credit, real output or labour preferences.
