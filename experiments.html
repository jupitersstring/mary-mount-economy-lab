Seventeen experiments and their conditional lessons

All results below are computed from version 2.0.0 with a fifteen-year horizon. Each row compares one shock with the same opening economy without that shock. This is a synthetic mechanism comparison, not an empirical policy estimate or a ranking of political systems.

Output, paid hours, net real hourly wage and real net liquid wealth refer to year fifteen. Equity refers to the year-zero value of the announced cash-flow path. Changes use the row’s own reference. Three debt experiments start with household debt of 40; the land-tax experiment starts with rent at 6% of GDP. Those prerequisites are explicit and must also be set in the interactive lab.

Robustness before interpretation

Repeat incidence with equal spending propensities. Reverse the income/substitution elasticities. Change the public-investment efficiency, innovation success and natural-rate assumptions. Hold operating flows fixed while changing valuation discount rates. An apparent lesson that disappears under another plausible closure is a conditional mechanism, not a fact about an ideology.

All seventeen displayed cases pass the goods-market, sectoral, bank and stock-flow closure checks. That is internal numerical qualification only. See equations and the theoretical research note.
